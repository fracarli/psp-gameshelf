# GameShelf presentation film

Format: horizontal 16:9, 1920 x 1080 delivery, approximately 45 seconds.
Primary destinations: LinkedIn and YouTube. Public voice, titles and subtitles
are English. Use real PSP footage for every demonstration of product behavior.

## Storyboard

| Time | Picture and action | On-screen title |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 00-04 | Two hands hold the PSP. Wake it and reveal the home menu. | Your PSP. A new way to play. |
| 04-09 | Navigate to Game > Memory Stick and highlight GameShelf. | Meet GameShelf |
| 09-17 | Open GameShelf. Show its real PSP startup sequence, press X to generate entries, then X to return home. | Build your game library |
| 17-23 | Open Memory Stick again. Begin a smooth push into the PSP display. | Your games, in the PSP home menu |
| 23-29 | End the push on the display only. Scroll through a few game covers; pause on one hardware-confirmed GBA game and let its background appear. | Covers. Backgrounds. Direct launch. |
| 29-38 | Press X once on that game's entry. Show the real loading sequence and the game becoming playable. | Choose a game. Press X. |
| 38-42 | Show a few seconds of real play, ideally matching the game's featured cover. | Less browsing. More playing. |
| 42-45 | Original bridge mark, GameShelf title and repository URL. | GameShelf — Your games. One home. |

The timing is an edit target, not a claim about startup duration. Adjust cuts
around the actual footage. For longer loading, extend the film or clearly
label a speed-up. Keep the final game selection, X press and first playable
frame continuous when practical. Do not manufacture a faster startup.

## Voice-over

>
> Meet GameShelf.
>
> Prepare your library, then return to the PSP home menu.
>
> Browse your games with covers and backgrounds.
>
> Choose a game. Press X.
>
> GameShelf opens the right emulator and loads your game automatically.
>
> Less browsing. More playing.
>
> GameShelf. Your games. One home.

Read with a calm, conversational tone. Leave space for navigation and loading.
Emulators remain part of the system; the benefit is skipping their manual
menus. Avoid claims that the PSP no longer needs emulator engines.

## Footage needed

A horizontal phone recording is sufficient; 4K is useful if available for the
screen push, but 1080p is acceptable. Record with the original audio and no
music, filters, captions or messaging-app recompression.

1. A continuous 45-90 second take covering hands holding the PSP, the home
   menu, GameShelf opening, entry generation, returning home, browsing,
   selecting a GBA game and reaching actual gameplay. Repeat slowly if needed.
2. A steady 10-15 second close shot of the screen while browsing the covers
   and waiting for a background to appear.
3. A continuous close shot from selecting the featured game through its
   complete load into gameplay. Include the whole loading interval.
4. Optionally, a clean photograph of the PSP held straight toward the camera
   for a thumbnail, and a voice recording of the script above.

Frame the entire PSP with room around it. Keep the display in focus, its four
corners visible, and avoid bright reflections. Move the camera slowly straight
toward the display or keep it still for a digital push. Hold the final position
for several seconds. Record a little extra before and after each action.

## Editorial notes

- PRO-C on this PSP may require FastRecovery after a full power-off. Start
  from standby with CFW active, or include that preparation accurately. Do not
  present a cold boot as proof of permanent firmware activation.
- GameShelf is needed when generating/updating entries. Already-generated game
  entries can be launched without opening GameShelf each session. A caption
  can say "Run GameShelf after adding games" during the setup shot.
- The PSP startup logo belongs where it actually appears in the footage.
  Do not add an artificial second firmware startup sequence.
- Keep the actual PSP content at its native aspect ratio. Animate a corner pin
  or perspective correction only when it matches the filmed display.
- Put captions in an outer band or unused space when possible; avoid covering
  the highlighted game title, PSP loading indicator or important gameplay.
- Use a game already confirmed on hardware. Do not advertise N64 performance
  from an integration that has not yet been tested on the actual PSP.
- Include a brief "PSP homebrew • Custom firmware required" footer in the
  introduction or end frame. Add upstream emulator credits in the video
  description. The public repository provides source and notices.
- Music is optional. Use original or appropriately licensed music, keep it
  low under the voice and retain a little real button/game sound.
